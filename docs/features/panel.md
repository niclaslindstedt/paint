# The right-hand panel, your way

The panel down the right-hand side is what you can do to the _drawing_ rather
than to a mark: the **Image** actions at the top, **Effects** and **Colour**,
and the **layer stack**. Not everyone uses all of it, and not everyone reaches
for it in the same order — so the arrangement is yours rather than the app's.

## Move a section

**Drag a section by its heading** and it goes where you drop it; the ones it
passes shuffle up or down to make room. The **grip** at the heading's left end is
the sign that says so, but the whole heading is the handle — a sixteen-pixel grip
is not something a finger can be asked to hit.

On a touch screen, **hold the heading for a moment first**, then drag. A quick
swipe still scrolls the panel, as it should, and a tap still folds the section
away — the press that picks a section up is the one you hold.

Out of the box the order is **Image, Effects, Layers, Colour**. Colour is under
the stack because it is what you do to a picture that is _finished_, while the
three above it are what you reach for mid-drawing — and the stack is the section
you open this panel for while you draw, so six adjustments you touch once should
not sit between you and it. If you work the other way round, drag it back up and
it stays there.

## Switch a section off

**Settings → Panel** is the same panel with its lid off: one row per section, in
the order the panel shows them, with the arrows that move a row (the keyboard's
way of doing what the grip does) and a switch that takes the section out
altogether.

Under each section are the things inside it, each with a switch of its own — the
**Resize** row, the **Flip** and **Mirror** pairs, every effect and every colour
adjustment by name, and the controls on the layer stack: the eye, the padlock,
the restack arrows, the bin and the **Merge layers…** row. Switch off the last
thing in a section and the section goes with it: a heading over an empty box is
not worth the room.

Two of them are worth reading the small print on. The **eye** and the **padlock**
are the only way back from a layer that is hidden or locked — and a drawing's
background sheet arrives locked — so switching either off leaves whatever is
already hidden or locked that way.

## The two that stay

**Image** cannot be switched off, and neither can **Start over** inside it. They
are not conveniences over something you could do another way: resizing the sheet,
turning it, mirroring it and emptying a drawing have no other route in the app,
and a panel switched down to nothing would leave a drawing with no way to change
its size. Their switches are there, dimmed, and say so.

## Switching layers off merges your drawings

Every other section here is a hiding: switch it off and the marks you made with
it, the layers you stacked and the effects you applied are exactly where you left
them, and switching it back on brings the controls back.

**Layers is the exception, and it asks first.** A sketchbook with no layers panel
is a sketchbook you cannot unhide a layer in, or unlock one, or restack one — so
switching the section off puts every drawing on a single layer: the background,
unlocked, holding everything that was on the stack (hidden layers included), and
where new marks now land. Undo brings the stacks back in one step. Switching
layers on again does not — it only brings the panel back.

Your arrangement travels with your settings, so a sketchbook you connect on
another machine opens with the panel set up the way you set it up here.
