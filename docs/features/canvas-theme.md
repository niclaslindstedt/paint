# The page, the ink, and the defaults behind them

A page that has chosen no colour of its own is **white**, and you draw on it in
**black**, in a light app and a dark one alike. That is a default rather than a
law: Settings → **General → Defaults** holds both colours, and either one can be
set to **Follow the theme** instead — which is what this app used to do on its
own, opening a dark sheet in light ink inside a dark app and flipping both back
when the app goes light.

Neither colour is written onto your drawings. They are resolved as a page is
painted, so changing one re-sheets or re-inks every drawing that never chose for
itself — including ones already drawn — and touches none that did. A page that
wants a colour of its own is given one when it is made (see below), because that
answer belongs to the drawing rather than to the app.

The same section names the **tool** you are handed and the **preset** it arrives
set to: out of the box the pen at its 0.5 mm liner. Those are not a view of
anything — they are what is put in your hand on a fresh install, and again when
you delete the last drawing and the app hands you a blank page to carry on with.

## Ink stays legible

Until you pick a colour from the toolbar, the ink is the default one — and, on a
page the default would vanish into, the ink that reads on it instead. Black on a
blackboard page is not a colour, it is a lost stroke, so a dark page draws in
white until you say otherwise. Once you pick a swatch, that colour is yours and
stays put on any page.

This applies to marks you have **already drawn**, too. A mark that never chose a
colour doesn't store one: it takes the page's ink whenever it is painted. So
changing the default ink — or flipping a whole sketch from dark to light —
re-inks it rather than leaving it invisible against the new page, and the marks
you deliberately drew in red stay red. Nothing in the drawing is rewritten; both
the default and the theme are ways of viewing it.

Eraser strokes never had a colour to flip: they take ink off rather than paint
the page over it, so a page flip finds nothing in them to re-ink.

## Pinning one drawing's colour

**New image → Page colour** is where a page gets one — a warm cream, a
blackboard — and it travels with the image and syncs with it, ignoring the
default and the app theme from then on.

It is asked there because it is part of what the page **is**, alongside its size
and its sheet, and because the stock swatches beside it are painted on it: pick
a black page and you are comparing the six surfaces as that black page rather
than as a stranger's white one. Like the size, it is fixed once the page exists.

## …or no colour at all

The **first swatch is a chequer, and it is what New image opens on**: no page. The marks land
on nothing, and the canvas draws the chequer under them — dark greys in a dark
app, light greys in a light one — so you can see that there is nothing there.

That is worth having as the default because an image is as often something to
drop onto a page somebody else owns as it is a sketch on a sheet of its own. A
PNG or an SVG of one downloads with nothing behind it without your having to
find a setting for it. (JPG has no transparency, so it is given the colour the
sheet would have had — see [getting work out](export.md).)

"No page" is the same thing as switching the **Background layer** off in the
layers panel, not a second idea beside it — so a page you started transparent can
be given a sheet later by switching that eye back on, and one you started with a
colour can have it taken away the same way. [More](layers.md).

An exported PNG always carries the page it was drawn on, dark sheet included.
